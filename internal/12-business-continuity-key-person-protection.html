02 · The Distinction

Continuity is not succession.

The two are routinely bundled into one conversation, and that is why neither gets done properly. They answer different questions, on different clocks, and they fail in different ways.

Succession planningBusiness continuity
Who leads the company next?

The planned transfer of leadership and ownership over years — choosing between family, internal management and an external buyer, preparing the successor, and formalising equity.		 How does the company keep operating through a disruption?

The unplanned loss of a person, a facility, a system, a supplier or a line of cash — and the pre-agreed steps that keep work moving.
Time horizon: three to five years.
Owner: co-founders, family, lawyer.
Failure mode: the company drifts, fragments among heirs, or sells for less than it is worth.		 Time horizon: the next 24 hours to 90 days.
Owner: managing director, operations, finance.
Failure mode: work stops, contracts are missed, cash seizes, clients leave.

Where they meet

They meet at exactly one point: the interim. The moment between "the founder is unavailable" and "the successor is ready" is governed by continuity, not succession. If that gap is undefined, the succession plan has no floor under it — a well-chosen successor can still inherit a company that has already lost its clients, its cash position and its best staff.

That is why this document exists separately. The roadmap's §07 sets out the succession process — Decide, Prepare, Formalise, Fund — and it is a sound process. It does not, and should not, contain the operating protocols that keep the business alive in the meantime. Those are the subject of this engagement.

Where this sitsThis document is a companion to the roadmap, not a replacement for any part of it. Section 07 of the roadmap keeps the succession strategy and its status. This document holds the continuity scope, and will hold the continuity plan once commissioned.
03 · The Risk Register

Seven ways the company stops.

These are the disruption scenarios that would interrupt DS Construction's ability to quote, fabricate, install and collect. They are ranked by how quickly they would bite, not by how dramatic they sound.

Scenario What it looks like at DS today What happens if it occurs Rating
1 · Key person — Daniel Sogbodjor Client relationships, pricing logic, supplier terms and final approval sit with the co-founder & CEO. No one below the two founders holds the complete picture. New quotations stop. Live clients wait for decisions. The pipeline freezes within days. Critical
2 · Key person — Gifty Sogbodjor The co-founder & Deputy MD carries a second share of operational and financial knowledge. There is no documented cover beneath either founder. Two simultaneous absences leave nobody with the full operating picture — the most likely real-world version of this risk. High
3 · Facility & plant Fabrication, plant, stock and work-in-progress are concentrated at the single Nima site. Fire, flood, a power failure or a closure order halts production and delays every live contract at once. High
4 · IT, data & communications Drawings, quotations, accounts and contacts are presumed to sit across personal devices and informal folders. Backup status is unverified. Device loss or ransomware removes the company's memory — and with it the ability to quote accurately or prove what was agreed. High
5 · Cash flow Receivables, supplier credit and project spend are managed by judgement rather than a rolling forecast. One delayed client payment or one loss-making project stalls payroll and material orders. High
6 · Supply chain Aluminium, glass and fittings depend on a small supplier set and import lead times. A single supplier failure or a currency move stops fabrication mid-project, with labour already committed. Medium
7 · Contracts & compliance Contracts, warranties, insurance and statutory certificates are held in paper files with no indexed register. A claim, audit or dispute cannot be answered quickly, and renewals are missed because nobody owns the calendar. Medium

Ratings are provisional and reflect exposure, not probability alone. A critical rating means the company has no functioning alternative if the scenario occurs. The register is re-scored in Week 1 against evidence from contracts, insurer documents, staff interviews and the accounts position.

04 · The Commissioned Scope

Eight components. One operating plan.

The engagement produces a single, usable continuity plan — not a policy manual. Each component ends in something a named person can pick up and act on the morning it is needed.

01
Key-person map & knowledge capture
Structured interviews with Daniel and Gifty Sogbodjor to document the relationships, pricing logic, supplier terms and project judgement that exist only in their heads — and to identify who else can hold each one.
02
Interim leadership protocol
A named acting arrangement with delegated authority, spending limits and a 30-day operating rhythm — so decisions can be made without waiting for a signature that is not coming.
03
Client & contract continuity file
An indexed register of every live client, contract, warranty, insurance policy and statutory certificate, with renewal dates, obligations and the named second contact for each account.
04
Cash-flow continuity
A 13-week rolling cash forecast, a defined minimum operating buffer, and pre-agreed trigger points that tell the company when to slow spend, chase receivables or draw on credit.
05
Facility & plant resilience
A single-site risk review covering fire, flood, power, security and plant failure, with a costed relocation and recovery option and the order in which production would be restored.
06
IT, data & communications
Verified backups, a documented access register held by more than one person, and a fallback for email, phone and drawing files that does not depend on a single device or inbox.
07
Supply chain continuity
A second-source map for aluminium, glass and fittings, with agreed lead times, credit terms and an emergency purchasing path that does not depend on the co-founders' personal relationships.
08
Crisis response & communication
A one-page activation card: who calls whom, in what order, and what is said to clients, staff and suppliers in the first 24 hours — including who speaks, and who does not.
What is deliberately excluded. This is not a legal succession instrument, a valuation, an insurance placement or an IT migration project. Where the plan identifies a need in those areas, it names the specialist action and hands it over — it does not attempt to perform it.
05 · Delivery

Four weeks, start to handover.

The plan is built in four working weeks, in parallel with the company's normal operations. Each week ends with a document the co-founders can read and challenge; the final week ends with a tested plan, not a draft.

WeekWorkOutput at week end
Week 1 Discovery & exposure mapping. Sessions with both co-founders, key staff interviews, review of contracts, insurer documents and the accounts position. Validated risk register and a one-page exposure map.
Week 2 Knowledge capture. Structured interviews, account and supplier mapping, collection of contract, cash and certificate data. Drafted key-person map and client & contract register.
Week 3 Protocol drafting. Interim leadership, cash triggers, facility and IT recovery options, supply second sources, crisis sequence. Drafted protocols and the one-page activation card.
Week 4 Test and hand over. Tabletop exercise with the leadership pair, correction of what fails, final plan and a board briefing pack. Signed continuity plan, activation card, board pack.

What DS Construction must provide

TimeFour sessions with Daniel and Gifty Sogbodjor, 90 minutes each; one hour with the workshop supervisor; brief access to two or three long-standing clients if they agree.
DocumentsLive client contracts, insurance policies, statutory certificates, the current accounts receivable and payable position, and any existing staff or supplier agreements.
AccessA working space for two days on site, and a named internal counterpart who can answer questions between sessions.
If something is missingIt is recorded as a gap in the plan, with an owner and a date — not treated as a reason to delay. Missing documents are themselves a finding.

The four-week schedule assumes decisions are available when asked for. If the co-founders' availability slips by more than a week, the schedule moves with it; the scope does not shrink.

06 · What It Protects

Before and after.

The measure of a continuity plan is not how it reads. It is whether a named person can act on it on a bad morning without calling either co-founder. These are the changes the engagement is designed to produce.

AreaTodayAfter the plan
Client relationships Held in the co-founders' phones and memory. An account file per client, with a named second contact and the history of what was agreed.
Interim leadership Undefined; decisions wait for the co-founders. A named acting lead with written authority limits and a 30-day decision rhythm.
Contracts & cover Paper files, no index, renewals unowned. An indexed register with renewal dates, obligations and a named owner for each.
Cash Managed by judgement, reviewed when pressed. A 13-week rolling forecast, a minimum buffer and pre-agreed spend triggers.
IT & data Backup status unverified; access concentrated. Tested backups, a dual-held access register and a fallback for email and drawings.
Facility Single site, no written recovery order. A costed recovery option and a stated order for restoring production.
Crisis response Improvised in the moment. One activation card: calls, order, messages, spokesperson.

How success is judged

Test 1 · The dry runThe leadership pair runs a tabletop disruption in Week 4 and reaches a decision without calling either co-founder. If they cannot, the protocol is rewritten until they can.
Test 2 · The document testA staff member who was not in the interviews can find the right procedure and the right name in under two minutes.
Test 3 · The 90-day checkAt 90 days, the register is current, the cash forecast has been maintained, and one live client has been serviced by the second contact.

This plan protects the company's ability to operate. It does not replace insurance, legal advice or the succession decision. It makes all three usable on the day they are needed.
